\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

MedSAM~\cite{ma2024medsam} adapts the Segment Anything Model~\cite{kirillov2023sam} to medical images and is routinely fine-tuned again for one target modality with LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} or visual prompt tuning~\cite{jia2022vpt}. In our earlier study~\cite{prior2026safer}, LoRA trained on dermoscopy gained in-domain accuracy but lost 0.18 Dice relative to zero-shot MedSAM on mammography, while decoder-only and full fine-tuning kept their far-OOD accuracy, and the loss correlated with how far the mask decoder's representations moved from the base model, measured by centred kernel alignment (CKA)~\cite{kornblith2019cka}. Throughout, \emph{OOD} refers to the shift between the adaptation data and the test data: near-OOD is dermoscopy from another clinic, far-OOD is another modality. MedSAM's pretraining covers all of these modalities, so far-OOD failure here is a loss of competence the base model had, closer to forgetting than to generalisation to an unseen modality. Beyond the known distortion of pretrained features by fine-tuning~\cite{kumar2022finetuning,tan2024protection}, we test whether far-OOD loss depends on \emph{where} in the segmentation pathway representations move. We ask three questions. (RQ1) Does anchoring decoder representations to the base model during adaptation reduce far-OOD collapse, and is the decoder specifically the place to anchor? (RQ2) When in the adaptation budget does the collapse appear, and what moves inside the model when it does? (RQ3) Can far-OOD failures be detected per image without ground truth?

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a CKA loss on an unlabelled OOD probe with a three-seed hook-placement study: encoder-only anchoring hurts, decoder anchoring repairs much of LoRA's far-OOD loss, both is best, yet decoder-only and full fine-tuning stay more robust;
  \item a budget study with drift curves and controlled LoRA variants that locate the collapse in joint encoder--decoder adaptation;
  \item a label-free per-image failure signal from decoder drift, with its scope: reliable for LoRA with tight prompts (AUROC 0.96, 0.80 and 0.69 over three seeds), not under loose prompts or for wholly retrained decoders, while the decoder's IoU head responds to poor prompts rather than to modality shift.
\end{enumerate}
