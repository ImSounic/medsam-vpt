\paragraph{Drift ranks LoRA's failures.}
Over three seeds LoRA fails on 61 to 82 of 647 BUSI images and 130 to 173 of 362 CBIS-DDSM images, and drift ranks these failures with AUROC $0.96 \pm 0.01$ and $0.80 \pm 0.02$ (Table~\ref{tab:detect}); on DMID (113 to 175 failures of 269) it scores $0.69 \pm 0.11$. Thresholds from 0.3 to 0.7 give 0.90 to 0.97 and 0.73 to 0.82 (seed 0). Flagging the 20 percent of BUSI images with the highest drift captures 87 percent of LoRA's failures and leaves 1.5 percent failures among the rest, from 9.4 percent overall (seed 0, supplement). LoRA's IoU estimate is at chance on all three sets ($0.51$ to $0.57$): after adaptation it collapses to a band between 0.4 and 0.6 regardless of mask quality. VPT failures are ranked almost as well on the ladder (0.92 to 0.95 on BUSI, 0.69 to 0.72 on CBIS-DDSM) but only at 0.55 to 0.57 on DMID.

\paragraph{Where the signal does not hold.}
Drift is informative when the decoder moves for some images and not others. It fails when the decoder is retrained wholesale (full fine-tuning on DMID, $0.57 \pm 0.01$; decoder-only, 0.39), when it is held in place by the CKA anchor (0.35 and 0.46 on DMID), so the anchor and the detector do not combine, and under loose boxes: with 50\,px jitter drift is below chance on CBIS-DDSM (0.23 to 0.36), while the IoU estimate detects the 220 to 236 failures of full fine-tuning and encoder-only LoRA with AUROC 0.75 to 0.79. The IoU head flags a poor box, which every model can sense, rather than a modality it has never seen. With 2 to 14 tight-box ladder failures, full fine-tuning and encoder-only LoRA hardly need a detector. Drift needs a base-model forward per image, and we did not compare it with base--adapted mask agreement or with dedicated failure detectors~\cite{zenk2024failure,valindria2017rca}.
