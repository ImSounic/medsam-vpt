\begin{abstract}
Parameter-efficient adaptation of MedSAM to one imaging domain can leave it worse than zero-shot on distant domains, a collapse that earlier work linked to drift of the mask decoder's representations from the base model. We intervene on that mechanism with a centred kernel alignment (CKA) loss that pulls the adapted decoder's activations on a 32-image out-of-distribution probe back toward the base decoder during LoRA training on dermoscopy. Over three seeds it raises tight-box Dice by 0.05 on breast ultrasound and 0.10 on mammography at no in-domain cost; anchoring the encoder alone lowers far-OOD Dice in every seed, and anchoring both halves gives the largest gain (0.18 Dice over unregularised LoRA on mammography). Across adaptation budgets from 50 to 2595 images, LoRA falls below zero-shot far-OOD at 250 images, exactly where its far-OOD decoder drift triples, whereas decoder-only and encoder-only adaptation never collapse and never drift far-OOD, even when they move the encoder more than LoRA does: the failure requires joint, unanchored adaptation. A held-out mammography set reproduces the ranking. Finally, per-image decoder drift detects far-OOD failures of parameter-efficient adapters with AUROC 0.95 on ultrasound and 0.82 on mammography, where the decoder's own IoU estimate is at chance, while that estimate is informative for full fine-tuning. Far-OOD robustness of adapted MedSAM is decided at the decoder, and it can be both protected and monitored there.

\end{abstract}
