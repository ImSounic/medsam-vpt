\begin{abstract}
Fine-tuning MedSAM on one imaging domain can leave it worse than zero-shot on distant domains. We adapt it on dermoscopy (ISIC 2018) and test on increasing shift from the adaptation data: dermoscopy from another clinic (PH2), breast ultrasound (BUSI) and mammography (CBIS-DDSM, and the held-out DMID). We add a centred kernel alignment (CKA) loss that keeps the adapted model's activations close to the base model on 32 unlabelled ultrasound and mammography images. Over three seeds, anchoring the mask decoder raises LoRA's Dice by 0.05 on ultrasound and 0.10 on mammography at no in-domain cost, while anchoring the encoder alone lowers far-OOD Dice in every seed. LoRA's far-OOD collapse coincides with a threefold jump in decoder drift between 50 and 250 training images, whereas decoder-only and encoder-only adaptation neither drift nor collapse. The anchor repairs LoRA but does not make it the most robust choice: decoder-only and full fine-tuning remain ahead far-OOD. Per-image decoder drift ranks LoRA's segmentation failures (Dice below 0.5) without labels, with AUROC 0.96 on ultrasound, 0.80 on mammography and 0.69 on DMID, but only with tight box prompts and for adapters that move the decoder together with the encoder. Far-OOD failures of adapted MedSAM are visible in, and can be reduced at, the decoder.
\keywords{Medical image segmentation \and Parameter-efficient adaptation \and OOD robustness \and Failure detection}
\end{abstract}
