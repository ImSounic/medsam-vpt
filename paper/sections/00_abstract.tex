\begin{abstract}
Parameter-efficient adaptation of MedSAM to one imaging domain can leave it worse than zero-shot on distant domains, a collapse that earlier work linked to drift of the mask decoder's representations from the base model. We intervene on that mechanism with a centred kernel alignment (CKA) loss that pulls the adapted decoder's activations on a 32-image out-of-distribution probe back toward the base decoder during LoRA training on dermoscopy. Over three seeds it raises tight-box Dice by 0.05 on breast ultrasound and 0.10 on mammography at no in-domain cost; anchoring the encoder alone lowers far-OOD Dice in every seed, and anchoring both halves gives the largest gain (0.18 Dice over unregularised LoRA on mammography). Across adaptation budgets from 50 to 2595 images, LoRA falls below zero-shot far-OOD at 250 images, exactly where its far-OOD decoder drift triples, whereas decoder-only and encoder-only adaptation never collapse and never drift far-OOD, even when they move the encoder more than LoRA does: the failure requires joint, unanchored adaptation. A held-out mammography set reproduces the ranking. Finally, without ground truth, per-image decoder drift from the base model ranks LoRA's far-OOD failures with AUROC 0.95 on ultrasound, 0.82 on mammography and 0.76 on the held-out set; flagging the 20 percent of ultrasound images with the highest drift captures 87 percent of failures, while the decoder's own IoU estimate is at chance and responds to poor boxes instead. Far-OOD robustness of adapted MedSAM is decided at the decoder, where it can be protected, and for LoRA its per-image departure from the base model is a usable failure signal.

\end{abstract}
