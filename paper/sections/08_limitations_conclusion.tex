\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:limits}

All models are adapted on dermoscopy, so we have not shown that the anchors help when a far-OOD set is the training domain. The drift curves and the full-data budget point are single-seed, far-OOD variance on mammography is large for every method, and the drift curves support the joint-adaptation account without being an intervention on drift itself. The CKA loss adds about 30 percent to training time, the drift signal needs a base-model forward and up to doubles inference cost, it is at chance for VPT on DMID, and its usefulness was established against ground truth offline. The regularised model inherits the base's sensitivity to loose boxes in-domain.

Within those bounds the evidence points one way. Far-OOD failure of adapted MedSAM arises where the mask decoder's representations leave the base model's, and it does so when encoder and decoder adapt together without an anchor; an anchor at the decoder removes much of the failure at no in-domain cost, an anchor on the encoder alone makes it worse, and for LoRA the size of the departure on a single image is a label-free signal of failure that holds on a domain used for nothing else. Protecting the decoder and monitoring it are two uses of the same quantity, the second with the qualifications above.
