\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:limits}

All models are adapted on dermoscopy, so we have not shown that the anchors help when a far-OOD set is the training domain. The budget and drift study is single-seed, far-OOD variance on mammography is large for every method, and the drift curves support the joint-adaptation account without being an intervention on drift itself. The CKA loss adds about 30 percent to training time, the drift detector up to doubles inference cost, and the regularised model inherits the base's sensitivity to loose boxes in-domain.

Within those bounds the evidence points one way. Far-OOD failure of adapted MedSAM arises where the mask decoder's representations leave the base model's, and it does so when encoder and decoder adapt together without an anchor; an anchor at the decoder removes much of the failure at no in-domain cost, an anchor on the encoder alone makes it worse, and the size of the departure on a single image predicts whether the prediction has failed. Protecting the decoder and monitoring it are two uses of the same cheap quantity.
